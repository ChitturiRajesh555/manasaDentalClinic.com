10 Best TMJ Exercises and Stretches for Pain Relief:

Exercise 1: Relaxed Jaw Exercise

This exercise is designed to encourage relaxation in the jaw muscles, which can be particularly beneficial for those experiencing TMJ-related tension and discomfort.

  1. Begin by finding a comfortable, quiet place to sit or stand.
  2. Gently rest your tongue on the roof of your mouth, just behind your front teeth. This position naturally promotes the relaxation of the jaw muscles.
  3. Allow your teeth to slightly separate while keeping your lips together. This creates a relaxed position for your jaw.
  4. Breathe deeply and hold this position for a few moments, focusing on the sensation of relaxation in your jaw muscles.
  5. Repeat this exercise several times throughout the day, especially during moments of heightened stress or when you notice jaw tension.

Exercise 2: Goldfish Exercises (Partial and Full Opening)

The Goldfish Exercise comes in two variations, partial and full opening, designed to gently mobilize the TMJ and strengthen the muscles involved in jaw movement.

Partial Opening:

  1. Place the tip of your tongue on the roof of your mouth.
  2. Place one finger on your TMJ (the area in front of your ear where the jaw moves) and another finger under your chin.
  3. Drop your lower jaw halfway and then close it. There should be a slight resistance but no pain.
  4. Perform this exercise 6-10 times, ensuring smooth and controlled movements.

Full Opening:

  1. Similar to the partial opening, but this time, open your mouth as wide as you can without causing pain.
  2. Use your fingers to monitor the movement and ensure it’s straight up and down.
  3. Repeat the full opening exercise 6-10 times, maintaining a gentle approach to avoid overextension.

Exercise 3: Chin Tucks

Chin tucks can help improve posture and relieve pressure on the TMJ by strengthening the muscles of the neck and jaw.

  1. Sit or stand with your spine in a neutral position.
  2. Gently pull your chin straight back, creating a “double chin” effect. This movement should not be painful or overly forceful.
  3. Hold this position for 3-5 seconds, then relax and return to the starting position.
  4. Repeat this exercise 10 times, focusing on the sensation of stretching and strengthening in the neck and jaw area.

Exercise 4: Resisted Mouth Opening and Closing

This exercise aims to strengthen the muscles involved in opening and closing the jaw, offering improved control and stability to the TMJ.

Resisted Opening:

  1. Place your thumb or two fingers under your chin.
  2. Gently open your mouth, pushing gently against your chin for resistance.
  3. Hold your mouth open for 3-5 seconds, then slowly close your mouth while maintaining the resistance. Repeat this resisted opening 5-10 times, ensuring the movement is controlled and pain-free.

Resisted Closing:

  1. Place your thumbs under your chin and your index fingers on the ridge between your lower lip and chin.
  2. Close your mouth as you gently press against your chin, providing resistance.
  3. Hold for 3-5 seconds, then relax.
  4. Perform this resisted closing 5-10 times, keeping the pressure gentle and consistent.

Exercise 5: Tongue Up Exercise

It may seem simple, but this exercise is likely to relax the muscles of the jaw; it also works in the alignment of the temporomandibular joint. Tongue Up Exercise:

  1. Place the tip of your tongue on the roof of your mouth, just behind your upper front teeth.
  2. While keeping your tongue in place, slowly open your mouth as wide as you comfortably can.
  3. Hold this position for a moment, then slowly close your mouth.
  4. Repeat this exercise 5-10 times, ensuring you maintain a gentle approach to avoid overstretching.

Exercise 6: Side-to-Side Jaw Movement

The side-to-side jaw movement helps enhance the flexibility and mobility of your jaw. Here’s how to do this exercise:

  1. Place a thin object, like a tongue depressor or a popsicle stick, between your front teeth.
  2. Gently move your jaw from side to side, keeping the motion small and controlled.
  3. Feel free to increase the thickness of the item as you get comfortable with the exercise between your teeth, such that you better test your jaw muscles.
  4. Perform this exercise for 5-10 repetitions per session.

Exercise 7: Forward Jaw Movement

  1. Forward Jaw Movement: Intended to help strengthen the muscles used to move it forward, and therefore may reduce pain from TMJ. Proceed as follows:
  2. The thin object should be placed between the teeth, and you should ask the patient to bring the lower jaw forward a little so that the upper teeth move ahead of the lower teeth.
  3. Hold this position for a few seconds, then return to the starting position.
  4. Repeat the movement 5-10 times, increasing thickness gradually between your teeth as the exercise becomes easier.

Exercise 8: Neck Stretches

If a person is holding neck and shoulder tension, this could add to a sore TMJ. Gentle neck stretches:

  1. Now, take your ear towards your shoulder by tilting your head to the side and holding it till a slight pull is felt on the other side of your neck.
  2. Hold for 15-30 seconds, then slowly return to the starting position and repeat on the other side.
  3. Perform forward and backward neck tilts, holding each position for 15-30 seconds.
  4. Do these stretches 2-3 times per side, ensuring you keep the movements slow and gentle.

Exercise 9: Shoulder Rolls and Stretches

Relieving shoulder tension can have a positive impact on TMJ symptoms:

  1. Begin by slowly rolling your shoulders in a circular motion, first forward then backward.
  2. Then stretch one arm across the chest, using the other arm to pull the arm closer towards your body while the shoulder muscles stretch.
  3. Hold each stretch for 15-30 seconds before switching sides.
  4. Repeat the rolls and stretches 2-3 times, maintaining a focus on relaxing the shoulder muscles.

Exercise 10: Deep Breathing and Relaxation Techniques

Incorporating relaxation techniques can enhance the benefits of TMJ exercises by reducing overall tension and stress:

  1. Find a quiet, comfortable space to sit or lie down.
  2. Close your eyes and take slow, deep breaths, inhaling through your nose and exhaling through your mouth.
  3. Focus on relaxing every part of your body, starting from your toes and moving up to your head.
  4. Spend 5-10 minutes on this relaxation exercise, using it as an opportunity to decompress and reduce stress, which can contribute to TMJ pain.

What causes TMJ disorders?

Teeth grinding or clenching (bruxism), jaw injuries or trauma, arthritis affecting the joint, misaligned teeth or bites, and chronic stress leading to muscle tension around the jaw are some of the common reasons that can cause TMJ disorders.

How do TMJ disorders lead to ear pain and tinnitus?

The temporomandibular joint is located close to the ear, and its dysfunction can cause referred pain to the ear. Additionally, TMJ disorders can affect the nerves and muscles connected to the auditory system, leading to ear pain and tinnitus (ringing or buzzing sounds).

Can TMJ disorders heal on their own?

Mild cases of TMJ disorders may resolve with rest and self-care, such as eating soft foods, avoiding jaw overuse, and practicing stress management. However, persistent or severe symptoms often require professional intervention, including physical therapy, dental appliances, or in some cases, surgery.

Are there specific exercises for TMJ pain relief?

Yes, targeted exercises can help strengthen and relax the jaw muscles. Some effective ones include:

  • Jaw stretches: Gently open your mouth as wide as comfortable, then close it slowly.
  • Resisted mouth opening: Place light pressure under your chin while opening your mouth.
  • Neck stretches: Stretch the neck to relieve tension in surrounding muscles.

Always consult a professional before starting a new exercise routine for TMJ disorders.
